Equivalents are metallurgical correspondences, not claims that our strip carries the foreign stamp. If your spec names an AISI or EN grade directly, send it — we will confirm what we can supply and what the mill can certify.

How hardness is decided

Tempering sets the number

The same strip can leave the furnace at 52 or 60 HRC. Softer holds an edge longer under impact, harder holds it longer under slicing — the right answer depends on how your customer uses the knife, and we quote the range with the offer instead of promising the maximum.

Measured on our own tester

Every tempered load is checked on the Rockwell tester and the reading is logged against the batch. A load outside the agreed range is re-tempered, not shipped.

Certified strip on request

We buy from TISCO (Taiyuan Iron & Steel), Baosteel, Jiuquan Iron & Steel and keep the mill test report with the heat number. Ask for MTR-backed supply when your market needs the paper trail.

What to send us instead of a grade name

The most useful brief is not "440C" — it is what the knife has to survive. Tell us the use, the price band and the market, and we will propose two grades with the trade-off written out.

  • Care dishwasher-safe required, or hand wash only
  • Target price FOB band per piece at your quantity
  • Paper trail MTR, food-contact or REACH / LFGB documents needed
